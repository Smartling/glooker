Four choices, and where we landed

Each one redraws the board above. What we propose is already selected — click any alternative to see what it costs.

01

Your epics have no Goal or Initiative above them. What should those two columns do?

The board devotes its leftmost 28% to the SPS hierarchy, and merges those cells vertically so a run of epics reads as one initiative. RND is flat — not one of your 25 epics has a parent — so as it stands you'd get two columns of em-dashes. Glooker currently drops parentless epics entirely, which is why pointing it at RND today renders nothing at all; that gets fixed either way.

02

The third tab is “Rollout”, which research never enters. Trade it for Backlog?

Rollout is a delivery stage; RND's workflow is Backlog → In Progress → Done / Rejected. Meanwhile six real hypotheses are sitting in Backlog with nowhere to appear — Translation Risk Profile System, Hyperlocalization, Source Assessment for Content Routing and three more. For a research board the queue is arguably the most interesting column on it.

03

You asked for about two weeks. We'd argue for keeping 30 days — and for counting rejected hypotheses as finished.

Two weeks turns out to be a sharper cut than it sounds, because research epics run for months rather than sprints. Of the six closed epics we checked, only RND-1191 (closed 5 Aug) falls inside a fortnight — RND-1152 at 29 days drops out, and the tab is left holding a single row. Thirty days, today's window, holds two. Separately, RND-968 Foundation Ranking v2 is Rejected, which Jira files under Done but leaves with no resolution date, so no window catches it at all. We'd include it by falling back to the last-updated date: a ruled-out hypothesis is a result, and a board that shows nothing happened is worse than one that shows the answer was no. Both of these are yours to overrule.

04

Half of each progress ring measures commits. What should it measure for you?

The ring is two arcs: the outer amber one is Jira closure, which works fine for you. The inner emerald one is commit volume against an expected rate, and the number in the middle is how many developers committed. Research does write code — Attila's commits carry RND-1185 prefixes — but the keys referenced tend to be standalone tasks rather than epic children, so little of it rolls up. Left as-is, the inner arc and the centre digit mostly report zero.

What this is and isn't

Worth being explicit, so nobody expects the half we're not building.

In

  • Research appears as a team, with its epics sourced straight from the RND project rather than inferred from who commits.
  • The same rows, rings, status tabs and inline Jira edits every other team's board has.
  • Due dates and status editable in place, writing back to Jira.
  • Parentless epics stop being silently discarded — a fix that helps every team.

Out, for now

  • Columns you can drag cards between. This is a table, not a kanban; board 646 stays the place to move work.
  • Ranking researchers against engineers by commit volume. That comparison isn't meaningful and we're not making it.
  • Any LLM summary of research work. The epic summaries other teams get lean on commit diffs.
  • Sub-task detail. The board shows epics; children roll up into the ring only.

What we need from you: a yes, or the single thing you'd change. If this isn't the board you had in mind, the most useful thing you can tell us is which part of 646 you'd miss.

Two places we've deliberately gone against your note, both reasoned above and both a one-line change to flip: keeping 30 days rather than two weeks, and counting rejected hypotheses as finished work.
